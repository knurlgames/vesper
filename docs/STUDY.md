# Study: what Banjo and Conker teach a 3D hub platformer

Written 2026-11-11, before any design. The goal: learn how Rare built these games, then plot ours.
**We take structure, never content.** No levels, characters, jokes, music or look from either game.

Sources were Wikipedia, banjocomet.com, Source Gaming, The Avocado, Nintendo Life, Superjump, the 2001 Official
Nintendo Player's Guide for Conker (archive.org), and Rare developer interviews. StrategyWiki, GameFAQs and the fan
wikis refused automated fetches. Numbers marked (unverified) come from memory, not a source.

## The decisions so far (owner, 2026-11-11)
- **3D**, with a **Banjo-style hub** and gated worlds. Conker is the reference for tone and action.
- **Set while Vesper is alive.** She runs a vigilante crew.
- **Vesper is the mentor, not the player.** Think Kakashi, Gojo, All Might. The player is a new character.
- **The Velvet Rope:** a secret society of Dommes who fight crime, **all women, all felines** (like the Amazons),
  Vesper at its head. Diverse in species, build and age, all clearly adult. The hero is a member, so she's a woman too.
- **The villain is a hypocrite boar:** the Land's loudest decency campaigner by day, flooding the clubs with
  contraband (mainly hypnotic sex toys) by night, to take over the Land (name pending).
- **Vesper dies at the end, on her own terms.**
- **Only linked to Nine Lives through Vesper.** Not part of the Infurno canon.

## 1. Banjo-Kazooie (1998)

**The hub (Gruntilda's Lair)** is a tower climbed floor by floor. It's the most linear part of the game: worlds hang off
it one at a time, and the music shifts as you near the next world.

**Two gates, both cumulative:**
- **Note doors** count notes held, never spent: 50, 180, 260, 350, 450, 640, 765, 810 (then four optional doors
  up to about 882). After the second door, each one asks for about 60–70 notes per world opened.
- **World puzzles** spend Jiggies: 1, 2, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 15 (69 in all), plus 25 for the final door, = 94 of 100.
  Late puzzles cost more than one world holds, so the player goes back to old worlds without being told to.

**Every world is the same checklist:** 10 Jiggies, 100 notes, 5 Jinjos (all five pay one Jiggy), 2 honeycombs.

| # | World | Theme | Moves taught | Transformation (tokens) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mumbo's Mountain | grassy tutorial | 3 | termite (5) |
| 2 | Treasure Trove Cove | beach | 2 | – |
| 3 | Clanker's Cavern | sewer, underwater | 1 | – |
| 4 | Bubblegloop Swamp | swamp | 1 | crocodile (10) |
| 5 | Freezeezy Peak | snow | 1 (unverified) | walrus (15) |
| 6 | Gobi's Valley | desert | the last move | – |
| 7 | Mad Monster Mansion | haunted house | – | pumpkin (20), needed to open world 8 |
| 8 | Rusty Bucket Bay | polluted ship | – | – |
| 9 | Click Clock Wood | one tree, four seasons | – | bee (25) |

- **The economy:**
  - Jiggies: 100, 94 needed (6 spare).
  - Notes: 900, 810 needed (10% spare).
  - Mumbo tokens: 115, 75 needed.
- **Moves are taught where they're first needed,** and teaching is done by world 6 of 9. Almost no Jiggy needs a move
  from a later world.
- **The hub has its own:** 10 of the 100 Jiggies, one Witch Switch per world that reveals a hub Jiggy, and a quiz on
  the game's own content before the final boss.
- **Length:** about 12 h for the story, about 15 h for everything.
- **What went wrong:**
  - The camera.
  - Notes reset when you died or left a world, and with only 10% spare that was the most hated rule in the game.
  - Worlds had no end-of-world beat (no bosses).

## 2. Banjo-Tooie (2000)

**The hub (Isle o' Hags)** is a wide outdoor island in areas, each opening as worlds do: a village, temple grounds, a
plateau, cliff tops, a wasteland, a quagmire. Teacher silos double as fast travel.
- **Worlds open on a cumulative Jiggy count:** 1, 4, 8, 14, 20, 28, 36, 45, 55, and 70 for the final door, out of 90
  (22% spare).
- **Moves are bought with a cumulative note count** (25 to 765), never spent.
- **Every world has** 10 Jiggies, 100 notes, a boss and a transformation.
- **The worlds connect:** a train between them, passages from one world into another, and Jiggies that need a move
  from a later world.
- **Length:** 16–17 h for the story, about 24 h for everything. That's twice Kazooie's length with fewer Jiggies, so
  rewards felt stingy.
- **What went wrong:**
  - "Excessive backtracking"; worlds too big to hold in your head (Rare's own Ed Bryan: "All the levels were so big").
  - Moves arrived faster than players could absorb them.
  - Enemies respawned too fast.
  - The frame rate.
  - Warp pads were reportedly bolted on after the levels were built, to patch navigation (unverified).
- **What went right:** bosses per world, depth, and worlds that feel like one place.

## 3. Conker's Bad Fur Day (2001)

**The structure: a hub (Windy) with chapters off it, each chapter linear.**

| # | Chapter | Set piece | Genre shift |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hungover | tutorial | – |
| 2 | Windy | the hub's own missions, a barrel ride down the hill | steering |
| 3 | Barn Boys | a hay monster | – |
| 4 | Bats Tower | flooded machinery, a chase | swimming |
| 5 | Sloprano | a boss fought as an opera | rhythm |
| 6 | Uga Buga | a mount, a nightclub, a race, an arena | racing, riding |
| 7 | Spooky | zombies, flying as a bat | shooter |
| 8 | It's War | a beach landing, a tank | shooter |
| 9 | Heist | a bank job, a final boss in a power suit | shooter |

- **The hub changes state.** Day, then night after the barrel ride, then a post-war Windy. The tutorial area comes
  back as the way into Spooky. The same map tells the story by changing.
- **Money is the gate,** paid to characters ($1,000 to the guards, $2,110 for the barrel). The official guide itself
  called them "otherwise meaningless checkpoints". Players found the overworld confusing.
- **Context pads replace a move list.** Stand on a pad and B does the one thing that spot needs: a slingshot, a
  shotgun, a blowtorch. The core kit (jump, hover, frying pan) never grows. That cut the tutorials, and the pads tell
  you what to do next. The cost: puzzles become trial and error, because the solution is handed over.
- **Each mission is a joke:** a setup, a playable task, then a punchline cutscene. The joke decides the mechanic (the
  opera boss, the poo-ball pushing), not just the dialogue on top.
- **Each chapter parodies a film,** with one big set piece. Rare's Chris Seavor called the beach landing "a game's
  worth in itself".
- **A dark ending that's earned.**
  - The game goes from day to night to war.
  - The person Conker loves is shot.
  - He's offered any fix by the programmers, forgets to ask for her back, and ends up king, alone, in the pub.
  - The crude tone makes the loss land harder, because the relationship was played straight.
- **Length:** 7–10 h, about 2 h of it cutscenes. It shipped about 20% shorter than planned after 4 years.
- **What went wrong:**
  - The camera.
  - Touchy platforming.
  - The shooter sections, which felt bolted on ("so unbelievably unnatural"), took most of the criticism.
  - Difficulty spikes, such as a race that took 20 to 30 tries.
  - A confusing overworld.
  - Edge gave it 7/10. It sold about 55,000 in its first month.

## 4. What our game takes, and what it avoids

### Structure
1. **A mid-sized hub that grows by area,** Tooie-style, but with Kazooie's sense of a path. Each area opens with a
   world, has its own teacher spot, and has fast travel **from the start**, not patched in.
2. **The hub changes state as the story moves,** like Conker's Windy. This is where the mentor arc lives: the city
   (or wherever the crew works) gets darker as the story nears what happens to Vesper.
3. **One cumulative count that's never spent opens worlds,** with about 15–20% spare. Later gates should cost more
   than one world holds. Show the per-world tallies on screen; never reset them on death.
4. **About 10 main collectibles per world, one per beat,** plus a set of five that pays one more. Players read a
   checklist they can finish.
5. **A boss at the end of every world.** Kazooie's worlds had no ending and Tooie fixed it.
6. **Mostly self-contained worlds.** One or two links per world back to the hub or a neighbour, never Tooie's web
   where a Jiggy needs a move from three worlds later.
7. **A recap finale** on the game's own content is cheap and memorable (Furnace Fun). Our version could be Vesper's
   final exam.

### Moves and the mentor
8. **Vesper is the move teacher**, the Bottles and Jamjars role. That's a gift: every lesson is a scene between
   student and mentor, and the mentor archetype is built on lessons.
9. **Teach a move where it's first needed, and finish teaching by about two thirds of the way,** so the last worlds
   test mastery. Kazooie's pace, not Tooie's flood.
10. **A fixed core kit plus context pads only for set pieces.** Never let a pad stand in for a decision the player
    should make.
11. **Hold back the mentor's power.** Like Kakashi's eye or Gojo's blindfold, Vesper shows what she can do rarely, so
    it lands each time. A world could end with her finally stepping in, or pointedly not.

### Action and tone
12. **Combat is first-class from day one.** Conker's weakest parts were its bolted-on shooters. If the hero fights,
    build that system before the levels, and use it in every world.
13. **Each mission is a joke with a mechanic in it:** setup, play, punchline. The jokes target criminals, hypocrites
    and the hero's dignity. The kink community and Vesper's crew are never the punchline (Nine Lives' rule).
14. **One big set piece per world,** budgeted like Conker's beach. Expect to cut 20%.
15. **Earn the dark turn.** Raise the stakes structurally (the hub changing, time of day, losses) and play the
    mentor and student bond straight, so the ending can hurt.

### Avoid
- An unreliable camera. It was the top complaint about all three games.
- Gates that are just fees. Every gate should be a character or a joke, and its cost should be visible.
- Required moves from later worlds, and respawning enemies that tax backtracking.
- Difficulty spikes in genre-shift sections (races, shooters). Give them their own tuned controls, or leave them out.

## 5. Scale, as a starting point
| | Kazooie | Tooie | Ours (proposal) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Worlds | 9 | 8 | **6**, plus the hub |
| Main collectibles | 100 | 90 | **70** (10 per world, 10 in the hub) |
| Needed for the final door | 94 | 70 | **about 58** (17% spare) |
| Bosses | 1 | 8 + 1 | **6 + the finale** |
| Length (story) | 12 h | 16 h | **8–10 h** |

Six worlds is the size Nine Lives' 3D side is already building at, so the scope is known.

## 6. Open questions for the owner
1. **The hero.** Who is the student? Species, why Vesper takes them on, what they're bad at. The mentor archetype
   needs a student with a lot to learn and one thing they're secretly good at.
2. **Crime fighter or assassin?** Vesper is "the one person who asks". A crew that restrains and delivers (rope,
   cuffs, hogtied crooks left for the police, played for laughs) fits her better than one that kills. But you said
   assassin too, and it's your call.
3. **Vesper's death.** Nine Lives players know she ends up in Purrgatory, so every scene carries it. Three ways:
   - **A. At the end.** The final world is the night it happens, and the hero can't stop it. Conker's ending, done
     straight.
   - **B. Mid-game.** All Might's decline or Gojo's sealing: she's taken out of the story two thirds of the way, and
     the hero finishes without her. The hub changes for good.
   - **C. After the credits.** She lives through the game, and the epilogue shows how she died, then her first day
     at a desk in Purrgatory's Lust district, filing her own form.
   - **My pick: A with C's coda.** She chooses how it ends, on her own terms, to cover the student. That's her
     consent theme carried to the end without being said aloud. The coda ties the two games together.
4. **The setting.** A city, given vigilantes. What kind (noir port, neon sprawl, a rotten old town), and what's the
   villain at the top?
5. **The crew.** Who else is in it? They could be the hub's residents, one per world, the way Nine Lives' bosses wait
   at their gates.
6. **The franchise.** Is this part of the Infurno canon (Gerald, the naming rules), or only linked through Vesper?
